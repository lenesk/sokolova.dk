## ChatGPT - a great tool to learn hundreds of examples
I wanted to share with you some thoughts on the strategy for boosting up your level of Danish, especially in the times, when we have a great tool like ChatGPT. It's a great tool, that you can use is an effective way to train your brain on multiply examples (you may estimate how many you need), something close to what machines are trained like. But it is also something addictive... like sugar, and is not always available at hand, or at an exam, unlike sugar. 

## 'Why so? - It's somehow artificial'
To be able to write and produce sentences without the help of ChatGPT requires some conscious control of your phrase, especially in Danish, where the sentence structure is fixed, and for non-native Danish speakers may be hard to get used to. So it's quite normal, that when you learn Danish you need an extra effort to word your sentence, and you may think 'Why so? - It's somehow artificial'. Can you recognize the feeling? 

For native speakers though the sentence structure is something they expect to be right, and it sounds wrong and broken in terms of rhythm and melody. If the wording of the phrase is not more or less correct, and thus the rhythmical pattern is broken, native speakers may misunderstand the phrase. Аnd you may think, it is because of your accent, wrong vowels… and many other things, but actually the rhythm and melody (the flow of stress and unstressed words) is what people perception and process. 

## What native speakers hear?

I am not a native speaker of Danish, and asked some natives speakers, what an inversion mistake sounds like. They told me, that a certain wording (word order) is a kind of a thing they expect. If you say e.g. ‘måske', then the native language perception is tuned to hear a verb. Of course, native speakers do think in parts of speech (e.g. a nouns, a verb…), but a particular part of speech triggers a particular rhythm due to the meaning, so a word order is not just a pure grammatical thing. It works in correlation with syntactical (sentence) stress and melody. 

## Learn like ChatGPT?

ChatGPT is based on language models, and is trained on millions of phrases where a prompt triggers a particular match, based on some probability rules in usage. If you want to train producing sentences like that with millions of examples, may be it is a good way to learn, but how long time will it take? It's my belief, that is is actually faster to understand what is behind this probability in usage - some few rules on the sentence structure and some tools to do so. Having a sample of a language in your learning phase, will boost your learning from the models you hear and read. In other words, you will extend your models. I do not think that ChatGPT has been developed without algorithms/models. So to learn knowing algorithms is faster then learning by hearing, at least for adults learners. 

## Issues you may solve

The challenge for learning to phrase a complex and correct sentence, is that many learners are already quite fluent to the level B1 like module 3 at DU3 for example. And many incorrect structures are automatized. It happens due to many factors. E.g., many language courses and books are based on the method of learning Danish via communicative approach, so it focuses on speaking without much analyzing what happens in a phrase in terms of functional grammar which is a good supplement for the commucicative method: e.g. how to logically connect the utterances in terms of contrast, explanation, cause and result (a common confusing in learnes). 

Some learners may feel stuck at level B1, and try several attempts passing writing at modul 4, drop out of module 4 at DU3 and then try to pass PD3 on their own. All because of a missing control to express themselves in a more complex way, when a common language producation is focused abround describing. Describing is what you learn at level A1 & A2. But expressing attitude to something at level B1 and B2 requires complexity. 

So to pass B2 (PD3) level exam you need to show a certain complexity of the language, it's simply one if the assessment criteria. Therefor passing PD2 does not automatically mean, that you can pass PD3 after some months of reading on previous exam papers for PD3, it is not at least common that such a strategy will work. A similar problem may arise when learners pass PD3, but not with the grades they want, because to pass PD3 with 7, 10 or even 12 as grades, you need to show even more complexity and detailed perceptive understanding of complex language (e.g. in reading and listening), as well as actively to produce it (in speaking and writing).

Learning through knowing rules is probably not a popular method today in language learning, but it proved so far to be one of the most secure, especially if you want to improve and grow in the language.
